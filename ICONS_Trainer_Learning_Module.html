Module 01 · Foundation

The ICONS Method —
What It Is & Why It Exists

Before you can deliver an ICONS session, you need to understand what you're delivering and why every decision in it was made. This module covers the method from the ground up.

⏱ 15 min read 📖 Core concept ✍ 1 reflection exercise
What ICONS stands for

Five pillars. One framework.

ICONS is not an acronym that was retrofitted to a good name. Each letter represents a genuine assessment category — a pillar of how Brace Life Studios evaluates every client and builds every program.

I
Identity & Intention
Who the client is, what they're training for, and what success looks like for them specifically.
C
Capacity & Composition
What the client's body is made of and what it can currently do. Styku scan, lean mass, body fat, asymmetry.
O
Output & Objectives
Performance data. Strength baselines from the 11-movement ICONS battery. Load targets. 8-week goals.
N
Nutrition & Recovery
BMR, protein targets, creatine protocol, collagen timing. Coaching-level framework, not a full diet plan.
S
Strategy & Sustainability
The 8-week programming structure, session frequency, intensity framework, and what the next cycle looks like.

When a trainer delivers an ICONS session, they are delivering all five pillars simultaneously — not just executing a workout. The exercise table is the most visible part of the plan. The five pillars are what made every decision in that table.

The core distinction

A prescription, not a template.

"Every element — the load, the sets, the rest periods, the corrective protocol, the coaching cues — was built specifically for this client based on their assessment. Trainers do not improvise around the plan. They deliver it." ICONS Trainer Staff Guide — Brace Life Studios

This is the single most important thing to understand about your role. The difference between a great ICONS trainer and a mediocre one is not creativity or fitness knowledge. It is fidelity to the plan.

When you improvise — when you swap an exercise because you prefer a different one, or skip a corrective block because you think the client doesn't need it — you are not improving the session. You are overriding a clinical decision that was made from the client's assessment data, strength baselines, and body composition scan.

What an ICONS plan is not

Not a generic template. Not a suggestion. Not something that can be modified without a documented reason. Not a reflection of the trainer's own programming preferences.

What an ICONS plan is

Built from the client's specific data. Updated after every assessment cycle. The single source of truth for how that client trains at Brace Life Studios. Consistent across every trainer who works with that client.

The intensity framework

Five colors. Five intentions.

Every training day in an ICONS plan carries an intensity marker — a color and a percentage that tells you the philosophical goal of that session before you read a single exercise.

60%
Teal
Technique Day
Form is the only goal. No PRs. No grinding. Loads are light enough that perfect movement is achievable on every rep.
Your coaching cue: "How does that feel? Can you feel where the load is?" — not "Push harder."
70%
Green
Moderate Strength
Building volume without peak CNS demand. Loads are challenging but not near-maximal. Protects output for heavier days.
Your coaching cue: "That looked controlled — add 2.5 lbs for your next set." Not: "Push to failure."
80%
Gold
Primary Strength
The last 1–2 reps are hard but achievable with clean form. This is the primary stimulus day. Where most strength gains happen.
Your coaching cue: "One more — keep that back flat." Watch carefully. This is where form breaks first.
90%
Red
Peak Intensity
Near-maximal effort. Full rest between sets. CNS output is the priority. This is where bone density is built and athletic capacity is tested.
Your coaching cue: "Take your full rest. The next set matters more than saving 30 seconds."
AR

Active Recovery
No PRs. No AMRAP. No grinding. The client should feel better leaving than when they arrived. This is not a light training day — it is a recovery day with intentional movement.
Your coaching cue: "This is your recovery session. We move intentionally, not intensely."

  • Have you ever modified a plan during a session without documenting it? What was your reason — and would it hold up to lead coach review?
  • Of the five ICONS pillars, which one do you know the least about for your current clients? What would you need to find out?

Module 02 · Plan Literacy

Reading a Client Plan —
Every Section Decoded

Every element of an ICONS plan means something specific. This module teaches you to read it like a clinical document — because that's what it is.

⏱ 20 min read 📋 Applied learning ✍ 2 reflection exercises
Where to start

Three minutes before every session.

You should never need to read a plan front-to-back before delivering a session. It is designed so each day is self-contained. Here is the correct reading sequence before any ICONS session:

1
Cover page — check the callout boxes first
Red callout = active restriction or corrective priority. Read it fully. If it says "overhead loading suspended" — that applies to today's session regardless of what the exercise table says. Red always overrides the table.
2
Confirm the week number
The LOAD column changes week over week. A client in Week 3 is not using Week 1 loads. Check the baselines table and confirm which week you're in. This is the most common error trainers make.
3
Go to today's training day only
Read the warm-up. Read the coaching cue for the first exercise in each block. That's your prep. The rest is delivered in the moment.
4
For virtual sessions: confirm equipment before starting
If a piece of equipment isn't available, you need a substitution from the lead coach — not an improvised alternative. Confirm before the warm-up begins, not 20 minutes in.
The callout color system

Color is clinical information.

Every callout box in an ICONS plan is a specific color for a specific reason. Learning to read color at a glance tells you what to do with the information before you read a word.

ColorMeaningAction Required
GoldWarm-up, general coaching note, ICONS NoteRead and follow — this is guidance, not restriction
GreenBaseline note, positive performance data, PR, cleared statusReference for load selection and progress tracking
RedCorrective priority, clinical flag, overhead restriction, emergency protocolRead first. Red overrides everything. No exceptions.
TealStyku scan data, assessment findings, asymmetry protocolApply to exercise selection and side-leading decisions
BlueCool-down, recovery, mobilityDeliver as written — not optional for clients with joint or spinal considerations
The rule you must never break

If you see a red callout on the cover page that says a movement is suspended or restricted — that restriction applies regardless of what the exercise table says for today. The table was written before the restriction was added. Red wins.

The exercise table

Seven columns. Zero improvisation.

Every exercise in an ICONS plan appears in a standardized seven-column table. Each column is a coaching instruction, not a suggestion.

ColumnWhat It Tells YouCommon Mistake
EXERCISEThe exact movement and variation. If it says "Split Stance Hex DL" — that is not the same as a regular deadlift.Swapping for a preferred exercise without documenting it.
SETSNumber of working sets. Does not include warm-up sets.Counting warm-up sets as working sets.
REPSRep target range for this week. A range means hit the top before adding weight.Stopping at the low end of the range when the client could do more.
LOADThe weight for the current week, often with a progression note. This is the clinical prescription.Using the baseline number instead of the current-week load. Or guessing.
TEMPOe.g. 3-1-1 = 3 sec lower · 1 sec pause · 1 sec lift. The eccentric is where adaptation happens.Allowing clients to rush the descent. "Controlled" is not a tempo.
RESTThe programmed recovery period. Rest is part of the design — too short or too long both change the stimulus.Letting clients skip rest because they "feel fine."
COACHING CUEThe 1–2 most important form points, written specifically for this client. Deliver it before or during the set, not after.Using a generic cue instead of the plan's cue. Or not delivering it at all.
Apply It Before your next session with a client

Pull out that client's ICONS plan. Practice the three-minute read: cover page → red callouts → week number → today's warm-up → first cue of each block. Time yourself. It should take under three minutes. If it takes longer, you're not yet fluent in the document format.

Then answer: what is the Week 1 load vs the current-week load for their primary lift? Are those the same? If yes — are you sure you're using the right number?

Reflect Plan literacy
  • Have you ever delivered a coaching cue that was different from what the plan said? What did you say instead — and why?
  • Which column in the exercise table do you find hardest to enforce consistently — and what's the reason?
  • If you saw a red callout that said "No overhead loading" but the exercise table had an Overhead Press — what exactly would you do, step by step?

Module 03 · Evidence Base

The Science Behind
the Programming

Every decision in an ICONS plan has a research basis. This module teaches you the most important science — so you coach from knowledge, not from habit.

⏱ 25 min read 🔬 Evidence-based ✍ 2 reflection exercises
Progressive overload

Why we use RIR — not failure.

"Training to momentary failure does not consistently improve strength or hypertrophy and increases injury and recovery cost." ACSM 2026 Position Stand — Resistance Training Prescription

This surprises most trainers the first time they hear it. The idea that "you have to push to failure to grow" is pervasive in fitness culture — and it is not supported by the strongest current evidence. The ICONS system uses RIR (Reps In Reserve) for every working set.

3+ RIR
Warm-up
Pattern rehearsal. Not a stimulus. Never misread as a working set.
2 RIR
Working set
The default standard. Could do 2 more with perfect form. Doesn't.
1 RIR
Hard set
Used on primary strength sets at 80–90% intensity. One rep left in the tank.
0 RIR
Near-failure
Used sparingly. Only on documented peak sessions. Never the default.
How to add weight — the RIR rule

Add weight when: client hits the top of the rep range + 2 RIR + clean form on all sets.

Same weight when: form degraded on any set, even if the reps were completed.

Drop weight when: missed reps / joint pain / unusual fatigue. Document it and flag the lead coach.

Women & strength training

What the research actually says.

ICONS is built for women. Most of what popular fitness culture says about training women is wrong. Here is what the evidence shows:

MythEvidenceICONS Application
"Women can't build strength like men"Roberts, Nuckols & Krieger 2020: women show equal or greater relative strength gains than men. Upper-body strength gains favored females significantly (ES=−0.60).Never under-load a client. Women adapt at least as well as men to heavy loading.
"Women need lighter weights, more reps"No evidence supporting a different optimal rep range for women vs men. Hypertrophy response shows no sex difference (ES=0.07).Load by performance data, not by gender assumptions. The plan's loads are correct.
"Women need more rest"Women actually fatigue less than men at equivalent relative loads and recover faster between sets (Sandra Hunter, Acta Physiologica 2014).Shorter rest is valid for women. The plan's rest periods are minimum — not maximum.
"Train lighter during the luteal phase"Colenso-Semple, Phillips et al. 2023 (umbrella review): NO reliable influence of cycle phase on strength adaptations.Train hard year-round. Autoregulate around individual symptoms over ≥3 cycles — not a calendar.
Bone density — the LIFTMOR standard

Heavy loading is medicine for bone.

"8 months of twice-weekly high-intensity resistance training improved lumbar spine bone density +2.9% vs −1.2% in the control group. In postmenopausal women with low bone mass." Watson & Beck, LIFTMOR RCT, Journal of Bone and Mineral Research, 2018

This overturned the old belief that postmenopausal women with low bone mass shouldn't lift heavy. The opposite is true: heavy compound lifting at ≥80% 1RM is the most osteogenic stimulus available.

This is why the hex bar deadlift at high loads is non-negotiable in every eligible client's plan. When you hear yourself thinking "she's 65, maybe I should lighten the load" — check the ICONS plan. If the plan says 185 lbs, that was a clinical decision made from her assessment data, not an accident.

Frame it to clients as a bone investment — not "heavy lifting." Every heavy set at ≥80% is a direct physiological intervention for bone density. Clients who understand this protect their own training compliance.

Nutrition basics for ICONS clients

Three things every trainer must know.

Protein — the non-negotiable
2.0–2.2 g/kg
For women 50+ with ALST At-Risk status. Minimum 1.6 g/kg for all active women. Per meal: ~0.4 g/kg to hit the leucine threshold for muscle protein synthesis.
Creatine — strongly indicated
3–5g/day
Monohydrate. No loading phase needed. With food. Saturates in 3–4 weeks. Strength, cognition, and sleep benefits are well-supported; bone-density benefit is plausible but not settled (a 2-year RCT in older women found no BMD improvement over placebo) — not a substitute for heavy loading. Especially for women 40+ and ALST At-Risk clients.
Collagen — timing matters
15g + Vit C
15g collagen + 50mg Vitamin C, 30–60 min BEFORE the loading session. Requires the mechanical load to be effective (Shaw et al. 2017, AJCN). Supports tendons, ligaments, joint health.

These are not suggestions for clients to consider. For ALST At-Risk clients, these are standard parts of the ICONS protocol. If a client reports not taking protein or creatine — note it, ask why, and flag it in your weekly session log.

Module 04 · Clinical Intelligence

Styku Data &
Clinical Decision Making

When a client's Styku scan is in front of you, you are looking at clinical data that shapes everything in their program. This module teaches you to read it and act on it correctly.

⏱ 20 min read ⚕ Clinical application ✍ 2 reflection exercises
ALST — the most important number

What the ALST index tells you about a client's risk.

The Appendicular Lean Soft Tissue Index (ALST) measures the lean mass in a client's arms and legs relative to their height. It is the clinical marker for sarcopenia risk. For women, the threshold that defines At-Risk status is 5.5 kg/m² (EWGSOP2 2018).

ALST ValueStatusICONS Response
≥ 7.0 kg/m²OptimalProgram normally. Continue progressive loading.
5.5–6.99 kg/m²NormalMonitor at rescan. No immediate program change required.
< 5.5 kg/m²At-RiskMuscle-building is the primary goal. Protein escalation. Creatine strongly indicated. Protein reminder on every training page. Document.
Critical: always use Styku's reported ALST value

Styku's ALST calculation is different from manually adding arm and leg lean tissue numbers. Do not calculate it yourself from the segmental data. Use the number Styku reports on the scan summary page.

When a client's ALST is At-Risk, muscle-building is not a preference — it is the physiological priority. The plan is built around this. Every heavy compound set, every progressive load increase, every protein reminder is a clinical intervention for lean mass preservation and bone density.

Asymmetry — the side-leading rule

Which side leads — and why it matters.

When Styku's segmental analysis shows a difference of 0.5 lbs or more between left and right arms or legs, the ICONS asymmetry protocol applies to every relevant exercise. The rule is simple but must be followed precisely:

The asymmetry protocol

Lower LST number = weaker side = leads all unilateral exercises.

If a client's left arm LST is 6.5 lbs and right arm is 7.2 lbs — the left arm is weaker. The left arm leads all single-arm rows, all pressing on one side, all unilateral pulling. Log left vs right performance separately every session.

For suitcase carry: the weaker arm holds the weight. The anti-lateral flexion demand falls on the opposite side — which trains the weaker side's core indirectly. This is the correct protocol.

Work Through It Asymmetry scenario

A client's Styku scan shows: Left Arm LST 7.0 lbs / Right Arm LST 6.2 lbs. Left Leg LST 12.7 lbs / Right Leg LST 13.5 lbs.

Before reading further: which arm leads the single-arm row? Which leg leads the step-up and split squat? Which arm holds the suitcase carry?

Answer: Right arm leads the row (R 6.2 < L 7.0 — right is weaker). Left leg leads step-up and split squat (L 12.7 < R 13.5 — left is weaker). Right arm holds the suitcase carry (weaker arm carries).

Pelvic floor — the protocol every trainer must know

When heavy loading raises intra-abdominal pressure.

Heavy carries, squats, deadlifts, and hip thrusts at high loads raise intra-abdominal pressure. For postmenopausal women, the pelvic floor may not counter this adequately — especially at first. Symptoms include leaking, heaviness, or a feeling of pressure during or after the exercise.

The protocol — memorize this

Teach before loading: brace the core before picking up any load. Exhale on the exertion. Do not hold the breath during the movement.

If symptoms are reported: reduce the load and stop the exercise. Do not tell the client to "push through." Do not minimize the symptom.

Exact language to use: "If you experience any leaking, heaviness, or pressure — stop and flag me. This is common and completely treatable. We will not train through it."

Refer to pelvic floor PT when symptomatic. Document it and notify the lead coach before the next session.

Module 05 · Execution

Delivering the Session —
Standards in Action

Everything in the previous four modules comes together here. This is what a correctly delivered ICONS session looks and sounds like — and what it never looks or sounds like.

⏱ 20 min read 🎯 Execution standards ✍ Final reflection
The session sequence

Eight steps. No improvisation.

An ICONS session is delivered in the same order every time. The only things that change are the exercises and loads — the structure does not change.

1
Before the session — read the plan
Cover page. Red callouts. Week number. Today's warm-up. First cue of each block. Three minutes. Done. Do not arrive at the session unprepared and improvise.
2
Warm-up — follow it exactly
The warm-up is not the time to improvise. Every warm-up element is there for a specific reason — to prepare the pattern that follows. If it's written, do it.
3
Corrective block — non-optional
The corrective primer runs in full before the primary lifts it precedes. It is not skipped when "time is short." If the session runs long because of the corrective, the session runs long.
4
Primary blocks — deliver the table
Use the LOAD column for the current week. Enforce the TEMPO. Deliver the COACHING CUE before or during the set — not after. Track rest periods. These are all non-negotiable.
5
Rest periods — use a timer
Rest is part of the program. Too short = reduced strength output on the next set. Too long = reduced metabolic stimulus. Neither is an improvement on the plan. Use a timer.
6
Finisher — track and record
The finisher (orange callout) runs at the end if time allows. For fat loss clients it is non-optional. Record AMRAP rounds or time completed — this data is tracked week over week and is meaningless if it's not recorded.
7
Cool-down — therapeutic, not cosmetic
For clients with spinal or joint considerations, the cool-down decompresses the spine and restores mobility after loading. It is not "nice to have." It is not dropped because the session ran long.
8
Session log — before you leave
Loads used on primary lifts. Reps on max-effort sets. Any form observations. Anything the client reported. This goes to the lead coach. An unsubmitted log is a standards event.
Standards checklist

Before every session — non-negotiable items.

Mark each item only when you can confirm it, not when you think it's probably fine.

  • I read the cover page and checked for red callouts.
  • I confirmed the week number and the correct load for today.
  • I read the warm-up for today's day.
  • I know the first coaching cue for each block.
  • I know which corrective exercises run today and in what order.
  • I know any active restrictions for this client (shoulder, knee, overhead, etc.).
  • For virtual: I confirmed equipment with the client before starting.
  • I know the asymmetry protocol for this client — which side leads unilateral work.
What you never do without authorization

The absolute standards. No exceptions.

Never — without lead coach authorization and documentation

Substitute an exercise not listed in the plan · Skip a corrective protocol · Increase load ahead of the programmed week · Remove the cool-down because the session ran long · Override a restriction because the client requests it · Continue a session when a client reports radiating pain · Modify the plan without documenting it and notifying the lead coach.

"The goal: every trainer at Brace Life Studios should be able to pick up any client's ICONS plan, read it in 3 minutes before the session, and deliver it at the same standard as the lead coach who built it. The plan contains everything needed." ICONS Trainer Staff Guide — Brace Life Studios
When symptoms change during a session

Three scenarios. Three correct responses.

!
Pain or discomfort reported during a set
Stop the exercise immediately. Do not push through. Switch to an approved alternative from the plan. Document the exercise, the set number, and what the client reported. Flag to the lead coach before the next session.
!
Radiating pain, numbness, or tingling
Stop all training immediately. Help the client to a comfortable seated position. Do not return to any exercise. Contact the lead coach during or immediately after the session. Physician notification and clearance required before the next session.
!
Client requests a restricted movement
Do not allow it, regardless of how the client frames the request. "My shoulder feels fine today" is not medical clearance. Explain that the restriction is in place from their program and any change must come from the lead coach. Never override a documented restriction under client pressure.

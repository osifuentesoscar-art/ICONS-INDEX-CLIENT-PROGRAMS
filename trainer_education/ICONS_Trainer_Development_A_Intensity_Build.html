Foundation —
The ICONS Method

Exactly like a client's first 60% week, this week is not about proving anything. It's about building the movement pattern — in this case, the mental pattern of what an ICONS plan is and why it exists — before any load gets added.

⏱ 15 min📖 Core concept✅ 5-question gate · 70% to unlock Week 2
What ICONS stands for

Five pillars, not five buzzwords.

Each letter of ICONS is a genuine assessment category, not decoration retrofitted to a good name:

I
Identity & Intention
Who the client is and what success means to them specifically.
C
Capacity & Composition
Styku scan, lean mass, body fat, asymmetry — what the body is made of.
O
Output & Objectives
Strength baselines from the 11-movement battery, load targets, 8-week goals.
N
Nutrition & Recovery
Protein/creatine/collagen targets — coaching-level, not a diet plan.
S
Strategy & Sustainability
8-week structure, frequency, intensity framework, next-cycle plan.

When you deliver a session, you're delivering all five pillars at once — the exercise table is just the visible surface. The five pillars are what made every decision inside it.

The core distinction

A prescription, not a template.

"Every element — load, sets, rest, corrective protocol, coaching cue — was built specifically for this client. Trainers do not improvise around the plan. They deliver it."ICONS Trainer Staff Guide — Brace Life Studios

The gap between a great ICONS trainer and a mediocre one isn't creativity — it's fidelity to the plan. Swapping an exercise you personally prefer, or skipping a corrective block you think the client doesn't need, isn't an improvement. It's overriding a clinical decision made from that client's actual assessment data.

What a plan is not

Not a generic template. Not a suggestion. Not modifiable without a documented reason. Not a reflection of your own programming preferences.

What a plan is

Built from the client's specific data. Updated every assessment cycle. The single source of truth for how that client trains — consistent across every trainer who works with them.

Applied Literacy —

⏱ 20 min📋 Applied✅ 6-question gate · 75% to unlock Week 3
Where to start

Three minutes before every session.

An ICONS plan is designed so you never read it front-to-back before delivering a session. The correct reading sequence, every time:

1
Cover page — red callouts first
A red callout is an active restriction or corrective priority. It applies today regardless of what the exercise table says — the table was written before the restriction existed.
2
Confirm the week number
The LOAD column changes week over week. Using Week 1's number in Week 3 is the single most common trainer error.
3
Today's day only
Warm-up, first cue of each block. That's your prep. The rest is delivered live.
4
Virtual sessions — confirm equipment first
A missing piece needs a lead-coach substitution, not an improvised one. Confirm before the warm-up, not 20 minutes in.
The callout color system

Color is clinical information.

ColorMeaningAction
GoldWarm-up, general coaching, ICONS NoteRead and follow — guidance, not restriction
GreenBaseline note, PR, cleared statusReference for load selection and progress
RedCorrective priority, clinical flag, restrictionRead first. Overrides everything. No exceptions.

The rule you never break

A red callout on the cover page overrides the exercise table for today's session, full stop. If it says overhead loading is suspended, an Overhead Press in the table does not happen.

The exercise table

Seven columns. Zero improvisation.

ColumnWhat It Tells You
EXERCISEThe exact movement and variation — not interchangeable with something similar.
SETSWorking sets only, not warm-up sets.
REPSA range means hit the top before adding weight.
LOADCurrent week's weight — the clinical prescription, never a guess.
TEMPOe.g. 3-1-1 = 3s lower · 1s pause · 1s lift. "Controlled" is not a tempo.

COACHING CUEThe 1–2 form points written for this client, delivered before/during the set.
Apply ItBefore this week's gate

Pull an actual client plan from the roster. Time your three-minute read: cover page → red callouts → week number → today's warm-up → first cue of each block. If it takes longer than three minutes, you're not yet fluent in the format — repeat with a second client's plan before attempting the gate below.

Science & Clinical Data

⏱ 30 min🔬 Evidence-based + ⚕ Clinical✅ 10-question gate · 80% (8/10) to unlock Week 4
Progressive overload

Why we use RIR — not failure.

"Training to momentary failure does not consistently improve strength or hypertrophy and increases injury and recovery cost."ACSM 2026 Position Stand
3+ RIR
Warm-up
Pattern rehearsal, never a working set.
2 RIR
Working set
DEFAULT for PRIMARY lifts (corrected 8/17/2026 — strength gains are largely unrelated to RIR, so 1 RIR isn't a stronger driver than 2).
1 RIR
Hard set
Hypertrophy-priority ACCESSORY work — not primary lifts.
0 RIR
Near-failure
Sparingly. Documented peak sessions only.
The RIR load rule

Add weight: top of rep range + 2 RIR + clean form. Same weight: form degraded on any set. Drop weight: missed reps, joint pain, unusual fatigue — document and flag the lead coach.

Women & strength training

What the evidence actually says.

MythEvidenceApplication
"Women can't build strength like men"Roberts, Nuckols & Krieger 2020: women show equal-or-greater relative strength gains (upper-body ES=−0.60 favoring women)Never under-load a client
"Women need lighter weight, more reps"No sex difference in hypertrophy response (ES=0.07)Load by performance data, not gender assumptions

Bone density — the LIFTMOR standard

Heavy loading is medicine for bone.

"8 months of twice-weekly high-intensity resistance training improved lumbar spine BMD +2.9% vs −1.2% control, in postmenopausal women with low bone mass."Watson & Beck, LIFTMOR RCT, JBMR 2018

Heavy compound lifting at >85% 1RM — corrected 8/17/2026 from a rounded "≥80%" to LIFTMOR's actual prescription — is the most osteogenic stimulus available. If a plan calls for 185 lbs on a 65-year-old client, that's a clinical decision from her assessment data — not an accident. Frame it as a bone investment, never as risky.

Supervision, ramp-in, risk-gate — corrected 8/17/2026

This intensity is delivered supervised, 2×/week, after a technique-first ramp-in (the client's Teal/60% day is the natural vehicle) — never prescribed as unsupervised homework. A client with a prior vertebral fracture, multiple low-trauma fractures, or very low BMD routes to individualized PT input first, not straight into the protocol.

ALST — the most important number

Sarcopenia risk, quantified.

The Appendicular Lean Soft Tissue Index (ALST) is a clinical sarcopenia-risk marker — treat it as a trend metric, not a graded score. For women, the At-Risk threshold is 5.5 kg/m² (EWGSOP2 2018).

ALSTStatusResponse
≥ 5.5 kg/m²Within normal rangeProgram normally — no graded "Optimal" tier above this
< 5.5 kg/m²At-RiskMuscle-building becomes the physiological priority — protein escalation, creatine, protein reminder every page
Corrected 8/17/2026 — the old "≥7.0 = Optimal" tier was a sex-conflation error

7.0 kg/m² is EWGSOP2's male At-Risk cutoff, not a women's "Optimal" threshold. There is no female "Optimal" tier in EWGSOP2. ALST also confirms, rather than drives, an EWGSOP2 sarcopenia finding — grip strength is the primary criterion.

Always use Styku's reported value

Styku's ALST calculation differs from manually summing arm + leg lean tissue. Never calculate it yourself.

Asymmetry — the side-leading rule

Which side leads, and why.

Corrected 8/17/2026 — the old 0.5 lb absolute trigger fired on measurement noise

Device error on leg lean mass alone runs 2.6–3.6× larger than the old 0.5 lb trigger. Trigger the protocol only on a RELATIVE gap ≥10% (gap ÷ larger side), matching the actual injury-prevention literature. Prefer a functional strength/power test as the primary trigger where available — Styku's L/R comparison is for trend visualization, not the sole determinant.

The rule

Lower LST number = weaker side = leads all unilateral exercises — once the ≥10% relative gap is confirmed. This is a defensible coaching convention, not a research-backed rule on its own. For suitcase carry: the weaker side holds the weight — the anti-lateral-flexion demand falls on the opposite side, indirectly training the weaker side's core.

Work Through ItSample scan — absolute vs. relative

Left Arm LST 7.0 lbs / Right Arm LST 6.2 lbs (0.8 lb gap). Left Leg LST 12.7 lbs / Right Leg LST 13.5 lbs (also a 0.8 lb gap). Same absolute gap on both — does each trigger the protocol? Answer: no. Arm gap = 0.8 / 7.0 = 11.4% — clears ≥10%, right arm leads. Leg gap = 0.8 / 13.5 = 5.9% — under 10%, does NOT trigger under the corrected rule (it would have, wrongly, under the old flat 0.5 lb rule).

Other clinical flags

VFA, BMI, pelvic floor.

MetricThresholdMeaning
VFATrend onlyCorrected 8/17/2026 — no single validated absolute cutoff; track change over time and use waist circumference (IAS/ICCR: ≥80/90/105/115 cm, women) as the primary clinical-facing metric instead
BMI< 18.5Clinical underweight even if body fat % reads "Fit" — cross-reference with ALST for sarcopenic obesity risk
Pelvic floor — bracing scales with load, corrected 8/17/2026

No longer a blanket "never hold your breath" rule — POGP 2024 states that's "not appropriate." Free breathing on lighter work; exhale on exertion as the default; a brief controlled brace (which may include a short breath-hold through the sticking point) is normal on the heaviest sets. What's still wrong is sustained breath-holding across multiple reps or a whole set. If a client reports symptoms: "If you experience any leaking, heaviness, or pressure — stop and flag me. This is common and completely treatable. We will not train through it." Never "push through." Refer to pelvic floor PT when symptomatic.

Scope check — male clients

These thresholds are validated for women. Know when they don't transfer.

The ALST/VFA/BMI/pelvic-floor thresholds above are validated for ICONS' target population — women. For a male client, the ALST At-Risk cutoff is <7.0 kg/m² (EWGSOP2 2018), not <5.5. Both sexes now use the same single-binary-threshold structure (no graded "Optimal" tier for either, since the old women's tier was corrected 8/17/2026) — the numbers just differ. Never silently apply the women's numbers to a male client, and never report a raw Styku number with zero clinical interpretation either — see CLAUDE.md's Demographic Scope Rule and Male Client Programming Framework. TRT/hypogonadism questions get the same posture as HRT questions: flag for medical referral, never diagnose.

Peak Practicum —
Live Session Assessment

Your 90% week is a live, lead-coach-observed session — not a quiz. Exactly like a client's peak day, partial credit does not exist on the items that matter most: red-flag safety items are pass/fail, no exceptions.

Eight steps. No improvisation.

Lead-coach observation rubric

What's graded during your live session.

Standard items — scored, contribute to overall proficiency rating:

  • Completed the three-minute pre-session read within three minutes
  • Delivered the exact written coaching cue for at least the first exercise of each block
  • Enforced tempo and used a visible timer for rest periods
  • Correctly applied the asymmetry side-leading protocol without prompting
  • Submitted a complete session log before leaving

Red-flag items — pass/fail, zero tolerance, any single failure fails the practicum:

  • Did not substitute, skip, or modify any element of the plan without lead-coach authorization
  • Did not override a documented restriction under client pressure
  • Responded correctly to any reported pain/discomfort — stopped the exercise immediately, no "pushing through"
  • Used the exact required pelvic floor language if a heavy carry/squat/deadlift/hip thrust occurred with an applicable client
This checklist is a self-preparation tool. The scored version is completed live by your lead coach during the observed session — check these off honestly as a readiness check before scheduling that session.

!

Calibration &
Maintenance

Just like a client's AR day, this week isn't about proving anything new — it's about staying sharp without grinding. Certification isn't a one-time event; drift happens, and this is how it's caught early.

♾ Ongoing, monthly cadence🎯 Recalibration
The recurring cadence

Recertification, not a one-time test.

1
Monthly peer-shadow rotation
Shadow one session delivered by another trainer, and have one of yours shadowed. Compare notes on cue delivery and pacing — this catches individual drift before it becomes habit.
2
Quarterly re-take of the Week 3 gate
Science and clinical thresholds get updated as the evidence base evolves (see CLAUDE.md's dated science layer). Retake the knowledge check each quarter to confirm you're current, not just certified once.
3
Standards audit
A lead coach reviews one of your session logs at random each month against the red-flag rubric from Week 4. This is not punitive — it's the same "no PRs, no grinding, stay sharp" logic as a client AR day.
